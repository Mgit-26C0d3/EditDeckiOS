Privacy policy

EditDeck has no accounts, no servers of its own, and no analytics. It collects nothing about you and sends nothing to me. Your data lives on your device and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud container under your Apple Account.

The short version

EditDeck is a local-first app. Everything you create is written to a store on your device. There is nothing to sign in to, no EditDeck server anywhere, and no tracking of any kind.

If your device is signed in to iCloud, EditDeck also mirrors your data into its own private iCloud container so it appears on your other devices. That container belongs to your Apple Account. I can't read it, and Apple's own privacy terms govern it.

What I collect

Nothing. EditDeck gathers no personal information, no usage data, no device identifiers and no crash telemetry. I run no servers and have no way to receive, see or store anything you enter into the app.

What is stored on your device

Everything you create in EditDeck is written to the app's own storage on your device:

  • Productions, schedules, bookings and calendar events
  • Financial records: rates, earnings, tax, expenses, receipts, mileage and work-from-home hours
  • Contacts and links you add
  • Notes, audio recordings and their transcripts, moodboards, imported files and Avid marker lists

None of it is sent to me or to any third party. The only place it travels is your own iCloud, described next. (Links you save can ask their own website for a title and an icon; that is covered under network requests.)

iCloud sync

EditDeck uses Apple's CloudKit and iCloud Drive to keep your data consistent across the devices signed in to your Apple Account. It writes only to EditDeck's private iCloud container, which means:

  • The data belongs to your Apple Account, not to me. I can't read it, list it or recover it.
  • Nothing is written to a public or shared CloudKit database, so it is never visible to other users.
  • Apple handles the transfer and storage under Apple's Privacy Policy, and the space it uses counts towards your iCloud storage.

Two mechanisms are involved. Your records, note images and receipts are mirrored through CloudKit. Audio recordings and files you import are carried by iCloud Drive instead, so they download when you open them rather than occupying every device. A few settings that belong to your library rather than to one device (whether you are registered for GST, your name, the folders a new production starts with, and work-from-home suggestions you dismissed) are kept in step through iCloud's key-value storage, also under your Apple Account.

Turning it off. There is no sync switch inside EditDeck; it follows your device settings. Turn off iCloud Drive for EditDeck under Settings › your name › iCloud, or sign out of iCloud, and the app runs entirely on the local store. EditDeck also falls back to a local-only store on its own whenever iCloud is unavailable.

Device permissions

EditDeck asks for a few system permissions so certain features can work. In every case the result is stored with your other data and is never sent to me or to a third party:

  • Camera, to photograph receipts and to take photos and scan documents into your notes and files.
  • Microphone, to record the audio notes you attach to notes.
  • Speech recognition, to write transcripts of your recordings. Transcription runs entirely on your device: EditDeck refuses to transcribe rather than send audio to Apple's servers, and no recording is sent anywhere to be transcribed.
  • Photos. EditDeck only ever sees the photos you pick to add, and it adds an image to your photo library only when you choose Save Image.
  • Contacts, only if you use it: to import the people you choose, and, when you turn it on, to save the contacts you add in EditDeck to your device's Contacts in a group called EditDeck. It reads only the people you choose and the contacts it saved itself.
  • Notifications, to show local reminders for your events, productions and backups. These are scheduled on your device; no notification is sent from a server of mine. Production reminders can show the rate and the expected pay; turn that off under More › Privacy › Amounts in reminders.

Reading a receipt (the amount, date and supplier) is done by text recognition on your device. The photo isn't sent anywhere to be read.

Backups

EditDeck can export a single .editdeck file holding your whole workspace, so you can keep a copy or move to a device that doesn't share your iCloud account. What happens to a backup you make is entirely yours to decide: where you save it, and whether you share it at all.

If iCloud Drive is on, EditDeck also makes an automatic backup once a week into its own folder in your iCloud Drive, keeping the latest four. It goes nowhere else. Turn it off under More › Backup & Restore › Back up to iCloud every week.

A backup file is not encrypted. It holds everything in your library, financial records included, so treat a copy you share the way you would treat the data itself.

Subscriptions

EditDeck is sold as a subscription through the App Store. Apple handles the purchase, the free trial, billing and cancellation; I never see your name, your payment details or your Apple Account. The app asks StoreKit, on your device, whether a subscription is active, and remembers the answer on that device so it can open offline. Nothing about your purchase is sent to me. Apple's handling of it is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.

Third-party code and network requests

EditDeck includes one open-source library, ZIPFoundation, which reads and writes the backup archive on your device. It is a file-handling component only: it collects no data and contacts no network.

There are no advertising, analytics, attribution or crash-reporting SDKs in the app.

Network requests. Apart from iCloud and the App Store, EditDeck makes one kind of request. When you save a link, it asks that link's own website for the page's title and the site's icon, the way a browser would; the site sees an ordinary visit from your device's IP address and nothing else of yours. A link that looks private (a review or download link, one carrying a key, or one on a file-sharing service) is never opened; only that site's front page is asked for its icon. Addresses on your local network are never contacted. Turn it off entirely under More › Privacy › Link previews.

Controlling and deleting your data

You can edit or delete any record inside the app at any time. Deleting EditDeck from a device removes the copy stored on it.

If you have been syncing, a copy remains in your iCloud account until you remove it yourself under Settings › your name › iCloud › Manage Account Storage. Because that data sits in your Apple Account rather than with me, you are the only person who can delete it, and there is nothing for you to request from me.

Children's privacy

EditDeck collects no personal information from anyone, including children. No data is gathered, transmitted or shared regardless of who uses the app.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted here with a revised date at the top. Because EditDeck still collects nothing, changes are expected to be rare.
